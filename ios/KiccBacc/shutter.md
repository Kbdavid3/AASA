# SHUTTER

A five-part choice serial. Second person. One night. Done by sunrise.

You work the night shift at a corner store. At 2:11 a.m. an envelope comes under the metal shutter. Your name is on it, spelled with the extra letter only your mother uses. Nobody at this job knows that name. The owner walks in at 6:00.

**Look:** blank face, two dot eyes, red store vest over a white t-shirt, black work pants, no hat, no bag. A flashlight. The city is fully drawn. The person stays simple.

**On screen every part:** `CLOCK` until 6:00, and `SEEN` — how many cameras can place you.

This draft follows one path so the five parts play as one night: **OPEN → TAKE → FOLLOW → TAKE →** the last choice is still open. Under each part is the other door, in case that side wins instead.

Post one part. The comments pick the word. The next part starts from the winner.

---

## Part 1 — Under the Shutter

**Arriving from:** the start of the night.
**Clock:** 2:11 a.m. · **Seen:** 0

### Narration

Two eleven. The coffee is old. The shutter is down. You are the only person on the block, which is the whole job.

Something slides under the metal. An envelope. Your name on the front, with the extra letter. You have not heard that version of your name out loud in years.

You open the flap with a thumb. A brass key. A locker number, 16, written on a bus-station claim stub. And a photo of this room, taken from the aisle. You are in it. You are looking down at the register. The timestamp in the corner is ten minutes ago.

You did not hear anyone come in. The bell over the door never moved.

You can slide the envelope back into the street and finish the cigarettes. Or you can put the key in your pocket and make the morning your problem.

### What we see

1. Wide shot of the store. One figure behind the counter. Pink neon from the sign outside, bent across the floor.
2. The shutter, from inside. A white envelope pushes through the gap and stops at your shoe.
3. Close on the name. One letter too many.
4. The key, the stub, the photo, laid out on the rubber mat of the counter.
5. The photo, larger. You, small in the aisle, head down. An empty space where the photographer stood.
6. The bell over the door, perfectly still.
7. Your hand, hovering. The envelope on one side. The key on the other.
8. End card, full frame.

### End card

**OPEN** or **RETURN**

Comment one word.

### This draft takes OPEN

The key goes in the vest. The photo stays on the counter, face down. You kill the lights and lock a door that someone has already been through.

### If RETURN wins

You push the envelope back under the shutter. At 2:40 it comes in again, wet, with a second photo: you, from outside, sliding it back. Part 2 is the street, not the station. The key still fits locker 16. They just wanted to see if you would touch it.

## Part 2 — Locker 16

**Arriving from:** OPEN
**Clock:** 3:02 a.m. · **Seen:** 1

### Narration

The bus station is too bright and smells like wet coins. Locker 16 opens on the first try. Nobody looks at a person opening their own locker. The camera does.

Inside, a paper grocery bag. Banded bills, used, not a movie stack. A folded list. Six names. Five crossed out in the same pen. Yours is last. Same extra letter. No line through it.

Under the cash, a phone with one contact saved: DO NOT ANSWER. It rings in your hand anyway. You do not pick up. It stops. It rings again.

At the end of the row, a woman in a gray coat is not looking at you. She is looking at the camera over your head. She shakes her head once. Small. The way a clerk tells you the soup is gone.

You can take the bag and walk out under that camera. Or you can leave it, turn the key, and pretend your name was a coincidence.

### What we see

1. The station, late. Rows of orange lockers. You, small, the red vest too bright for the room.
2. The key turns. The door sticks, then gives.
3. The bag, the bands, the list. Five lines killed. One left alive.
4. Your name at the bottom. The pen is still dark, not dry.
5. The phone, face up on the cash. DO NOT ANSWER. The screen lights.
6. The woman at the end of the lockers. One shake of the head. Eyes on the camera, not on you.
7. The camera, a white dome, with you and the open locker in its eye.
8. End card.

### End card

**TAKE** or **LEAVE**

Comment one word.

### This draft takes TAKE

The bag goes under the vest. The phone stays in the locker, still ringing, so whoever is calling thinks you are still standing there. You are already on the stairs.

### If LEAVE wins

You shut 16 and keep the key, which is worse than keeping the bag. The phone keeps ringing inside the metal. Part 2 becomes the walk home, and the woman follows at a distance, not to take the bag. To see whether you are smart enough to be number six, or only scared enough.

## Part 3 — The Warning

**Arriving from:** TAKE
**Clock:** 3:40 a.m. · **Seen:** 2

### Narration

She catches you at the taxi stand. No hello. She talks like someone timing a light.

"Five names took a bag. Five names got a visit before sunrise. You are six. The phone rings so they know the locker opened. You left it ringing. That bought you minutes, not a pardon."

She will not touch the bag. She says her hands have already been seen with the others, and the others do not get a second night. She wants you to carry it one more stop. A storage building by the river. Unit 12. If the bag never goes inside, your name gets the line through it.

Behind her, a dark car idles with the lights off. The driver is a shape. The phone, back in the locker, is no longer the thing ringing. Her phone is.

You can follow her and bet that number six is the one they still need alive. Or you can run with the bag and find out what the visit looks like from the inside.

### What we see

1. The taxi stand. Rain on the pavement. You, the bag held low, the vest showing.
2. She steps into your path. Gray coat. Hands in her pockets, empty on purpose.
3. Cut to a quick, silent flash: five crossed names, each with a door behind it. No faces. Just doors.
4. Her phone, buzzing. She does not look at it. She looks at your vest, where the bag breaks the shape.
5. The dark car. Lights off. Exhaust moving.
6. Unit numbers, imagined, painted on a river wall: 12, larger than the others.
7. Two ways out of the frame. Her, toward the river. The street, away.
8. End card.

### End card

**FOLLOW** or **RUN**

Comment one word.

### This draft takes FOLLOW

You do not give her the bag. She does not ask to hold it. She walks half a block ahead, like a stranger who happens to be going the same way. The dark car does not follow her. It follows you.

### If RUN wins

You cut through the station garage and come out on the street behind the store. The car is already there, patient, lights off. Part 4 is not the warehouse. It is your own shutter, up, and the visit happening in the store whether you attend it or not.

## Part 4 — Unit 12

**Arriving from:** FOLLOW
**Clock:** 4:26 a.m. · **Seen:** 3

### Narration

Orange doors. One light that buzzes. She stops at the corner of the building and will not come closer. "If I walk in, the camera inside learns my face with yours. You are the one they invited."

Unit 12 is unlocked. The bag in your vest suddenly feels like a ticket someone else bought.

On a folding table: a hard drive, a cheap camera on a tripod, and the photo from the store, printed, pinned, labeled with tonight's date. There is a chair facing the lens. There is tape on the arms. Not used yet. Waiting on a size.

This is the visit. It was never going to be a conversation.

The drive is still recording. A red light, no bigger than a seed. Somewhere above you, a metal door rolls up, slow, the sound of a person who has done this enough times to stop hurrying.

You can take the drive and kill the record of your face. Or you can leave it running and be gone before the chair gets an occupant.

### What we see

1. The river building. A long row of doors. She stays in the dark at the corner and points, once.
2. Unit 12, ajar. A slice of hard light.
3. The table. Drive, camera, the photo of you at the register.
4. The chair. Tape on both arms, ends curled, ready.
5. The red recording light. Your blank face, tiny, in the lens.
6. The ceiling. The roll-up door moving, an inch at a time.
7. Your hand over the drive. The door behind you, still a way out.
8. End card.

### End card

**TAKE** or **LEAVE**

Comment one word.

### This draft takes TAKE

You pull the drive. The red light dies. You leave the bag on the chair, because walking out with both is how you get remembered. The roll-up door is a third of the way open when you hit the rain.

### If LEAVE wins

You back out and leave the drive recording an empty chair. She is gone from the corner. The bag is still under your vest, and the camera now has a clean shot of you deciding not to touch the only thing that could erase you. Part 5 starts with that footage already on its way to someone else.

## Part 5 — Open Shutter

**Arriving from:** TAKE
**Clock:** 5:49 a.m. · **Seen:** 4

### Narration

You make it back. The corner is blue, the kind of morning that has not started. The shutter you locked is up. The lights are on. The coffee pot is fresh. Someone brewed it for a shift they do not work.

Your vest is on a hanger behind the counter. You are still wearing yours.

On the register, the envelope is back. Empty. Under it, a new photo: you, at locker 16, the bag under the vest, an hour ago. On the back, a time. 6:00. And one line, in the same hand as your name.

"Put the drive on the counter and clock in. Or take it and do not be here when I am."

The owner is not due until 6. Someone is already in the stockroom. A box settles on a shelf, carefully, the sound of a person trying not to announce themselves.

The drive is warm from your pocket. The vest on the hanger is your size.

### What we see

1. The store from the street. Shutter up. Too much light for 5:49.
2. The coffee pot, full, dripping. You did not set it.
3. The hanger. A red vest, identical, waiting.
4. The envelope on the register, empty. The new photo beside it.
5. The back of the photo. `6:00.` The line of handwriting.
6. The stockroom curtain. A shadow shifts. A box touches a shelf.
7. Split frame. The drive on the counter. You, at the door, the real vest still on.
8. End card. No winner written. The night ends when they comment.

### End card

**STAY** or **GO**

Comment one word.

### If STAY wins

You set the drive on the counter, hang your own vest over the other one, and face the curtain. Part 6 starts in the stockroom, one minute before the owner arrives, with the person who spelled your name correctly standing between the shelves.

### If GO wins

You keep the drive and leave through the alley. The vest stays on the hanger like you finished the shift. Part 6 starts on the first bus, with the phone from locker 16 ringing in a stranger's hand, and your uncrossed name already on a new list.

## How to shoot a part

Each part is about a minute of voice. Eight pictures. Animate three: the envelope sliding in, the locker opening, the roll-up door, or the walk away. The rest can be stills with a slow push.

The last card is only the two words, big, and "comment one." Whichever word is ahead when you lock the vote, that is the next part. This file already tells you the first scene of either winner.
